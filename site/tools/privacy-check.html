What your browser reveals

Every website can read a surprising amount without asking permission. This page reads those values from your own browser and explains which ones help identify a device. It sends nothing anywhere — there is no server to send them to.

What this means

On their own, none of these values gives away who you are. Together they form a fingerprint. Combinations of screen size, timezone, language, installed fonts and graphics card are often distinctive enough to recognise the same browser on a return visit — even after cookies are cleared, and often across different sites.

This is why “we do not collect personal data” and “we cannot identify you” are different claims. A fingerprint is not a name, but it can link visits.

Reduce your exposure

  • Update your browser. Newer versions round or standardise more values and split trackers from the page.
  • Block third-party requests. A tracker cannot fingerprint you if its script never loads — that is what the BrowserGuard extension's tracker blocking is for.
  • Use the browser's privacy settings for cookie blocking and tracking protection. These are more reliable than any single extension.
  • Do not chase a perfect score. Blocking some signals can make a browser rarer, and therefore more identifiable. Consistency with a mainstream browser is often better than aggressive spoofing.
  • Separate contexts. Use a private window or a separate browser profile for sensitive accounts.

What is not shown

This page does not read your IP address (that would require a server), your browsing history, your keystrokes, your files, or the content of other tabs. Browsers do not permit it.
